# Executive Summary

## Ecommerce Sales Analysis Using SQL

**Analysis Period:** March 16, 2026 – June 14, 2026  
**Tools:** PostgreSQL, SQL, VS Code

## Project Overview

This project analyses an ecommerce dataset containing 40,000 orders from 10,000 registered customers, covering customer activity, revenue performance, payment outcomes and the product catalogue.

Using SQL, the analysis explores the business's overall performance, identifies revenue patterns and evaluates customer purchasing behaviour to derive actionable business recommendations.

## Key Business Findings

### 1. Revenue and Business Performance

The business generated **₹28.29 crore in paid revenue** across 37,822 paid orders, with an overall Average Order Value (AOV) of ₹7,478.70.

April recorded the highest revenue at ₹12.31 crore. Revenue fluctuations were primarily driven by changes in paid order volume, while AOV and average basket size remained relatively stable.

### 2. Customer Retention Drives Revenue

Among 8,438 customers with at least one paid order, 3,759 (44.55%) were repeat customers, while 4,679 (55.45%) made only one paid purchase.

Despite representing less than half of purchasing customers, repeat customers contributed **87.63% of total paid revenue**. Their average observed spending was ₹65,938.72, compared with ₹7,479.28 for one-time customers.

This highlights the importance of repeat-purchase conversion and customer retention.

### 3. High-Frequency Customers Are Major Revenue Contributors

The VIP segment consisted of 238 customers (2.82% of purchasing customers) who completed at least 21 paid orders during the observation period.

This segment generated **₹8.16 crore, contributing 28.83% of total paid revenue**. Regular customers contributed another 26.55%.

Together, VIP and Regular customers accounted for 55.38% of total paid revenue, making retention and engagement within these segments important marketing priorities.

## Business Recommendations

- **Improve customer retention:** Prioritize repeat-purchase campaigns and second-purchase conversion among one-time buyers.
- **Protect high-value segments:** Use loyalty rewards and personalized engagement to retain VIP and Regular customers.
- **Monitor revenue drivers:** Track paid order volume, AOV and basket composition to understand changes in revenue performance.
- **Measure campaign effectiveness:** Monitor repeat purchase rate, customer revenue and second-purchase conversion.

## Data Limitations

The analysis covers approximately three months, from March 16 to June 14, 2026. March and June are partial months, so direct comparisons with complete calendar months should be avoided.

Customer counts also depend on the measurement used: the initial business overview considers all order activity, while customer segmentation considers only customers with paid orders.

The available period is insufficient to establish long-term growth patterns, seasonality or true customer lifetime value.

## Conclusion

The analysis indicates that repeat customers are the primary contributors to ecommerce revenue. Revenue performance is closely associated with paid order volume, while a relatively small group of high-frequency customers contributes a substantial share of revenue.

The business should focus on retaining valuable customers, converting one-time buyers into repeat purchasers and monitoring paid order performance to support informed marketing and business decisions.
