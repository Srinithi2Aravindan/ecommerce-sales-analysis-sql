# Business Health Analysis

## 1. Revenue Overview

The ecommerce business recorded total paid revenue of **282,859,232.18** across **37,822 paid orders** during the analysis period from March 16, 2026, to June 14, 2026.

The overall Average Order Value (AOV) was **7,478.70**.

The analysis considers only orders with a `paid` payment status to ensure that failed payments are not included in revenue calculations.

## 2. Monthly Revenue Trajectory

|    Month   | Paid Orders |     Revenue    |    AOV   |
|------------|------------ |----------------|----------|
| March 2026 |   9,111     | 67,565,766.34  | 7,415.85 |
| April 2026 |   16,352    | 123,101,390.78 | 7,528.22 |
| May 2026   |   9,716     | 72,768,051.16  | 7,489.51 |
| June 2026  |   2,643     | 19,424,023.90  | 7,349.23 |

Revenue peaked in April at 123,101,390.78, supported by 16,352 paid orders. Compared with March, April revenue increased by 82.19%, while paid order volume increased by 79.48%.

In May, revenue declined by 40.89% and paid orders declined by 40.58%. This indicates that changes in order volume were the primary driver of monthly revenue fluctuations.

March and June are partial months because the dataset begins on March 16 and ends on June 14. Therefore, they should not be directly compared with complete calendar months.

## 3. Average Order Value (AOV) Analysis

The overall AOV was 7,478.70, while the median order value was 4,597.28. The difference between the average and median indicates that higher-value orders pull the average upward.

Monthly AOV remained relatively stable:

- March: 7,415.85
- April: 7,528.22
- May: 7,489.51
- June: 7,349.23

April recorded the highest AOV, increasing by only 1.52% compared with March. May AOV decreased by just 0.51%, despite the significant decline in revenue.

This suggests that the business's revenue fluctuations were driven more by changes in the number of paid orders than by substantial changes in average spending per order.

## 4. Basket Composition Analysis

The business sold 100,400 items across 37,822 paid orders, resulting in an average of 2.65 items per order.

Monthly average item quantities remained consistent:

- March: 2.65 items per order
- April: 2.66 items per order
- May: 2.65 items per order
- June: 2.67 items per order

Average merchandise revenue per item was 2,386.29 across the analysis period. April recorded 2,399.67 per item, compared with 2,365.50 in March.

Both average items per order and average revenue per item showed relatively small changes. This reinforces the finding that order volume, rather than major changes in basket size or item value, was the main driver of revenue fluctuations.

Note: Merchandise revenue per item is calculated using order item line totals divided by quantities. It is different from order AOV, which uses the total order amount.

## 5. Data Quality and Business Limitations

The revenue reconciliation check confirmed that the sum of monthly paid revenue exactly matched the overall paid revenue of 282,859,232.18, with a difference of zero.

The analysis also identified the ten highest-value paid orders for further review. Their order totals were consistent with the recorded subtotal, tax, shipping fee and discount components. However, additional product-level investigation would be required before classifying any high-value order as unusual.

The dataset covers approximately three months, from March 16 to June 14, 2026. March and June are incomplete calendar months, limiting direct monthly comparisons. The available period is also insufficient to establish long-term growth patterns or seasonality.

## 6. Overall Business Assessment

The business generated substantial paid revenue during the observed period, with April recording the highest monthly revenue and paid order volume.

Monthly revenue fluctuations were primarily associated with changes in paid order volume. Average order value, average items per order and average merchandise revenue per item remained relatively stable.

The key business observation is that maintaining and improving paid order volume may be important for sustaining revenue performance. Further analysis over a longer period is recommended before drawing conclusions about long-term business growth or seasonality.
